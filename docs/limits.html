Numerical Limits and Scientific Use

Matrix inversion uses floating-point elimination with partial pivoting. When an intermediate becomes non-finite, inversion retries after row scaling; it throws if the scaled calculation or final inverse remains non-finite. A nonzero pivot does not guarantee an accurate inverse for an ill-conditioned matrix; check residuals and coordinate scaling for the application. Determinants may underflow or overflow at extreme scales. If elimination overflows, determinant calculation retries with row scaling; this can recover finite values, but extreme differences within a row can still underflow during scaling and ill-conditioned results may be inaccurate. Metric construction checks for a finite inverse instead of using the determinant product as its singularity test; it can still reject extreme inputs whose inverse cannot be represented.

Volume-form and dual calculations obtain the determinant sign and square-root magnitude from pivot logarithms, allowing some cases where the determinant itself underflows. Elimination overflow triggers a row-scaled retry. They reject an unrepresentable volume scale; scaling and intermediate tensor products can still underflow or lose precision.

These are computational tools, not a validated scientific package. Curved-space derivatives generally use central finite differences; explicit first- and second-order forward paths are available for selected field operators; geodesics use fixed-step RK4; retarded times use bisection; and WebGPU uses Float32. RetardedField returns a four-potential, not acceleration or electric and magnetic field strengths. Choose coordinate scales and step sizes deliberately, test convergence, and compare results with analytic cases. Maxwell evolution uses periodic, time-staggered Yee-style forward/backward differences and preserves up to seven matching leading batches within the one-million-history-component cap. Spatial field components share one JavaScript array shape; gridCoordinates exposes their conventional staggered positions, and collocation provides linear interpolation to common nodes and times. General PyTorch device and autograd behavior is not implemented. Pedagogical visualization models are labeled as such.

Versioned JSON interchange currently covers selected algebra, spacetime and mechanics value types listed in the algebra guide; other spacetime objects, object-valued frame metadata, callbacks and GPU resources do not yet have a supported interchange format.
