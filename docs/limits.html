Numerical Limits and Scientific Use

Matrix inversion uses floating-point elimination with partial pivoting. A nonzero pivot does not guarantee an accurate inverse for an ill-conditioned matrix; check residuals and coordinate scaling for the application. Determinants may underflow or overflow at extreme scales. Metric construction checks for a finite inverse instead of using the determinant product as its singularity test; it can still reject extreme inputs whose inverse cannot be represented.

Volume-form and dual calculations obtain the determinant sign and square-root magnitude from pivot logarithms, allowing some cases where the determinant itself underflows. They reject an unrepresentable volume scale; intermediate tensor products can still underflow or lose precision.

These are computational tools, not a validated scientific package. Curved-space derivatives use central finite differences; geodesics use fixed-step RK4; retarded times use bisection; and WebGPU uses Float32. RetardedField returns a four-potential, not acceleration or electric and magnetic field strengths. Choose coordinate scales and step sizes deliberately, test convergence, and compare results with analytic cases. Maxwell evolution uses periodic, time-staggered Yee-style forward/backward differences and preserves up to seven matching leading batches within the one-million-history-component cap. Spatial field components share one JavaScript array shape rather than carrying component-specific Yee offsets, and PyTorch device/autograd behavior is not implemented. Pedagogical visualization models are labeled as such.
